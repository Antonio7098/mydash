# HSBC-inspired visual standards

These are project defaults derived from the requested My Dashboards visual
direction. They are not a substitute for an official internal brand manual.
Never claim formal HSBC brand approval or compliance without an approved source.

## Character

The interface should feel:

- calm;
- precise;
- professional;
- restrained;
- spacious;
- trustworthy;
- contemporary without being fashionable.

Avoid decorative excess, novelty dashboards and dense “control room” styling.

## Colour

Default palette:

```text
Primary accent    #DB0011
Canvas            #FFFFFF
Primary text      near-black / charcoal
Secondary text    restrained neutral grey
Borders           pale neutral grey
```

Use red selectively for identity, active state and important emphasis. Do not
turn every heading, card or metric red.

Never use colour as the only carrier of status. Pair it with text, iconography
or shape.

## Typography

- Prefer a clean system sans-serif stack unless an approved font asset exists.
- Use a small number of sizes and weights.
- Make hierarchy obvious before adding decoration.
- Keep body copy readable and plain.
- Avoid all-caps paragraphs and excessive letter spacing.

## Layout

- Use generous whitespace.
- Align to a consistent grid.
- Keep primary actions visually obvious.
- Prefer a few strong groups over many bordered cards.
- Use solid surfaces for information and translucency only for lightweight
  navigation or framing.
- Let content determine card dimensions; avoid uniform tiles when the material
  has different needs.

## Dashboards

- Lead with the decision or operational question.
- Put the most important summary first.
- Use charts only when they reveal a comparison, distribution, trend or
  relationship more clearly than text.
- Include units, dates and source context.
- Avoid decorative gauges and unexplained scores.
- Make empty, loading and error states intentional.

## Presentations

- One primary idea per slide.
- Use short, declarative titles.
- Prefer evidence and diagrams over paragraphs.
- Keep repeated chrome minimal.
- Preserve a clear narrative from context to implication to action.

## Navigator

The navigator should remain extremely minimal:

- white canvas;
- small top-left HSBC mark from approved assets;
- compact expandable navigation;
- category selector near the top centre;
- miniature artefact previews;
- solid title/action panel beneath each preview;
- restrained glass treatment only on the preview mount;
- no heavy application header.

## Accessibility

- Maintain readable contrast.
- Use semantic landmarks and headings.
- Support keyboard navigation and visible focus.
- Provide text alternatives for meaningful images.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Do not rely on hover for essential information.
- Keep touch targets usable.
- Test narrow and wide viewports.

## Assets

Use approved assets from the repository library. Do not redraw the HSBC mark,
extract logos from screenshots or invent brand graphics.

When no approved asset exists, use a neutral placeholder and state that an
approved asset is required.

The seeded Core asset `mydash-brand-mark` is an approved **project fallback**,
not an HSBC logo. It may be used in internal prototypes until an approved
internal HSBC asset is supplied. Do not describe the fallback as an HSBC mark.
